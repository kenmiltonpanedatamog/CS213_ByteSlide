### Boolean Algebra
Boolean Algebra is a branch of mathematics introduced by George Boole in 1847 where the values of the variables are the truth values *true* and *false*, usually denoted as 1 and 0 respectively. Instead of basic arithmetic operations like addition and multiplication, its primary operations are logical conjunction (AND), disjunction (OR), and negation (NOT).

This binary logic system is the fundamental mathematical bedrock of all modern computer science. It provided the exact theoretical framework needed to design digital circuitry; every physical microchip, logic gate, and high-level programming language conditional statement (like "if/else" commands) operates entirely on the principles of Boolean Algebra.
